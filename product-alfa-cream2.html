Key Components & Features

Bowl
The cream separator consists of a bowl or chamber where the milk is poured for separation. The bowl usually has a conical shape to facilitate the separation process.
Centrifugal Force
Cream separators work on the principle of centrifugal force. When the milk is poured into the bowl and spun rapidly, the heavier milk particles are forced outward, while the lighter cream particles move toward the center.
Separation Discs or Plates
Inside the bowl, there are typically separation discs or plates that aid in the separation process. These discs help to create turbulence and increase the surface area for efficient separation of cream from milk.
Spout or Outlets
Cream separators have separate outlets for milk and cream. The cream, being lighter, collects near the center and is drawn out through a separate spout or outlet. The skimmed milk, which is heavier, is drawn out through another outlet located near the periphery of the bowl.
Adjustable Settings
Some cream separators may feature adjustable settings to control the speed of rotation or the thickness of the cream obtained.
Capacity
Cream separators come in various capacities, ranging from small hand-cranked models suitable for home use to large industrial machines capable of processing large volumes of milk.
Material
Cream separators are typically made of stainless steel or food-grade plastic to ensure hygiene and durability.
Ease of Cleaning
Removable parts and smooth surfaces make cream separators easy to clean and maintain. Some models may be dishwasher-safe for added convenience.
